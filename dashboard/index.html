How it works

A world model is a differentiable simulator

Every step compresses an observation into a latent state, learns the dynamics of that state, and predicts reward + continuation — so a policy can be trained by dreaming forward inside it, with no real environment steps.

ML in depth

The concepts actually doing the work

Every ML core here — the RSSM, the ELBO, the imagination rollout, the DAgger loop — was implemented from scratch, not imported. This is the reference sheet: the concept, the exact formula used in this codebase, and why it's load-bearing rather than decorative.

Variational inference — the ELBO

log p(x|a) ≥ E_q[log p(x|z,a)] − KL(q(z|·) ‖ p(z|a)) loss = MSE(ô,o) + MSE(r̂,r) + BCE(ĉ, 1−done) + β·KL(q‖p)

The world model's training objective. Marginalizing over the latent is intractable, so a posterior q(z|h,e) gives a tractable lower bound instead of the true likelihood.

Why it matters: the KL term is the price of using an obs-informed posterior instead of the blind prior — minimizing it is literally what teaches the prior to predict the latent without seeing the observation. Delete it and imagination has nothing to run on.

Reparameterization trick

z = μ + σ ⊙ ε, ε ~ N(0, 1)

Rewrites a stochastic sample as a deterministic function of the network's outputs plus external noise.

Why it matters: a raw sample from N(μ,σ) has no gradient. This is what lets an imagined λ-return backprop through the sampled latent, through the dynamics, and into the actor's weights.

RSSM: split deterministic + stochastic state

h_t = GRU(h_{t−1}, z_{t−1}, a_{t−1}) prior: p(z_t | h_t) posterior: q(z_t | h_t, e_t)

State is a pair (h, z), not one vector. h is a sample-free GRU memory; z is a small latent for what's uncertain this step.

Why it matters: the prior is what runs at imagination time, when there's no observation to condition on — the posterior only exists to train it. Collapsing this into a plain RNN removes the calibrated-uncertainty channel the policy needs.

Free bits — the anti-collapse guard

KL_term = max( KL(q‖p), free_bits ) (per latent dim)

Floors the KL penalty instead of letting it shrink all the way to zero.

Why it matters: with no floor, gradient pressure pushes the posterior to just copy the prior and ignore the observation entirely — posterior collapse. Watch KL and reconstruction together: healthy training keeps KL meaningfully above zero while recon improves.

Model-based RL via imagination

actor_loss = −mean(V^λ) − entropy_coef · mean(entropy)

The actor and critic never touch the real environment — they train entirely on rollouts dreamed by rolling the RSSM's prior forward.

Why it matters: because the world model is differentiable, maximizing the return backprops straight through the reward head and dynamics into the actor — a value gradient, not a REINFORCE-style policy gradient. It's also exactly why a wrong model gets exploited instead of just under-performing (see the case study below).

λ-returns — the bias/variance dial

V^λ_t = r_t + γc_t·[(1−λ)v_{t+1} + λ·V^λ_{t+1}]

Bootstraps imagined rollouts (short, horizon-limited) with the critic's own value estimate beyond the horizon.

Why it matters: λ=0 is one-step TD — low variance, biased by the critic. λ=1 is Monte Carlo — unbiased, high variance. λ=0.95 (used here) interpolates between them.

DAgger — dataset aggregation

roll out π_current → expert relabels every visited state → aggregate → retrain (repeat)

Fixes plain behavior cloning's core flaw: a policy trained only on the expert's states has never seen its own mistakes.

Why it matters: DAgger's no-regret guarantee comes from training on the learner's own visited distribution, not the expert's — closing exactly the distribution-shift gap that took plain BC's route completion from ~3% to ~23% on the first round alone.

DART-style recovery data

executed = expert_action + triangular_impulse(t; t0, τ, σ) label = expert's CLEAN action

Drives with the expert but injects a triangular steering perturbation so the car drifts off-center, then records the expert's correcting action as the label.

Why it matters: generates the "when off-center, steer back" signal off-policy, with no live expert needed to intervene — the covariate-shift fix DAgger formalizes, applied without an interactive expert.

Controlled dynamics ablation

cfg.dynamics ∈ {"rssm", "mamba"} — one interface, swap only the recurrence, re-score identically

GRU-based RSSM vs. a minimal Mamba-style selective state-space model, behind the exact same prior/posterior interface.

Why it matters: isolates whether the recurrence choice — not the heads, not the training loop — is what limits dynamics modeling, instead of confounding it with a full architecture rewrite.

Where the world-model latent helps, and where it's a bottleneck

A controlled ablation found that behavior-cloning the expert inside the RSSM latent loses lane/heading signal; cloning directly from the raw 259‑dim state does not. That single finding is why the driving policy below bypasses the world model entirely — the world model is still used for prediction and the failure case study on this page.

Honest case study — not hidden, not fixed

When imagination lies: the model-exploitation failure

Dreamer's whole trick is training a policy by imagining rollouts inside a learned world model, with zero real environment steps — the return backpropagates straight through the dynamics into the actor. The catch: if the world model is wrong anywhere, the actor learns to exploit that error instead of learning to drive.

Here, a policy trained inside a world model built from random, crash-prone MetaDrive data converges to a degenerate action — full steering lock, full throttle — that the model rates as a great plan and reality rates as catastrophic:

Degenerate policy this run steer = 1.00 · throttle = 1.00

The world model itself learned the observation fine in this same run (reconstruction loss 245 → 0.20) — this is not an undertrained encoder. It's the actor finding a corner of latent space the model never saw real consequences for, and optimizing straight into it.

The documented fix is Dreamer's iterated loop — collect with the current policy, retrain the world model on policy-visited states, repeat — which grounds the model where it actually drives. A follow-up attempt at full DreamerV3-style stabilizers (return normalization + symlog value heads) hit real NaNs from an unbounded scalar head and was reverted rather than shipped as a fragile stand-in — logged honestly as an open problem, not smoothed over.
